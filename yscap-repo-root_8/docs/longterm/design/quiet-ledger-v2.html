How to read this
“You are ahead after” is the month your money comes back. B and C cost more on the day you close and less every month after it; divide the extra closing cost by the monthly saving and that is the month the two cross. Before that month the cheaper closing wins; after it, the cheaper payment does. The mirror also happens: an option that pays you at closing and charges a higher rate keeps you ahead until that month, and behind from then on.

re-derived by hand

You asked me to check them rather than trust them. Both are correct. The incremental cost reproduces both of your own worked examples to the digit, and the break-even reproduces by hand in both directions and refuses in the three cases where no answer exists.
1   The effective cost of the extra borrowing
( bigger × its rate  −  smaller × its rate )  ÷  ( bigger − smaller )
It is built on interest, not on the payment — a P&I payment is partly your own capital coming back, so annualising it prices your money back to you as a cost. And it counts the re-pricing of the whole loan, which is the question actually being asked.
